## Learn a few, well

There are hundreds of shortcuts. A handful cover most of the time you spend with the mouse. Learn three this week, use them until they are automatic, then add three more.

The shortcuts below are for Excel on Windows. The [shortcuts page](/excel-shortcuts/) lists these and many more, with the Mac versions.

## Moving and selecting

- **Ctrl+Arrow** jumps to the edge of the data in that direction.
- **Ctrl+Shift+Arrow** selects from the current cell to the edge of the data.
- **Ctrl+Home** goes to A1. **Ctrl+End** goes to the last used cell.
- **Shift+Space** selects the row. **Ctrl+Space** selects the column.
- **Ctrl+Page Down** and **Ctrl+Page Up** move between sheets.

## Writing formulas

- **F4** cycles a reference through $A$1, A$1, $A1 and A1.
- **F2** edits the cell and shows which cells the formula reads.
- **Alt+=** inserts a SUM of the cells above.
- **Ctrl+Enter** puts what you typed into every selected cell at once.

## Filling and copying

- **Ctrl+D** copies the cell above into the selection below.
- **Ctrl+R** copies the cell to the left into the selection to the right.
- **Double-click the fill handle** copies a formula down as far as the column next to it has data.

## Working with lists

- **Ctrl+T** turns a list into an Excel table.
- **Ctrl+Shift+L** turns filters on and off.
- **Ctrl+1** opens Format Cells.

## Checking your work

- **`` Ctrl+` ``** shows formulas instead of results.
- **Ctrl+[** jumps to the cells a formula reads.
- **Ctrl+Z** undoes the last change, and **Ctrl+Y** redoes it.

## How to make them stick

Put a list of your three current shortcuts next to the screen. Each time you reach for the mouse to do one of them, stop and use the keys instead. After a week, they are habit.
