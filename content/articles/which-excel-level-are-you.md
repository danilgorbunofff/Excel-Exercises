## Why it is worth knowing

Job adverts ask for "intermediate" or "advanced" Excel without saying what that means. The lists below are a common reading of each level. If you can do everything in a list without looking it up, you are at that level.

## Beginner

- Enter data, format numbers and dates, and adjust columns.
- Write formulas with `+`, `-`, `*` and `/`.
- Use SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX and COUNT.
- Copy a formula down a column.
- Sort and filter a list.
- Make a simple chart.

**Next step:** absolute references with `$`, and IF.

## Intermediate

- Use absolute and mixed references on purpose.
- Write IF with AND or OR, and choose between nested IF and IFS.
- Total and count with SUMIFS and COUNTIFS on several conditions.
- Look up values with XLOOKUP or VLOOKUP with an exact match.
- Clean text with TRIM, LEFT, MID and SUBSTITUTE.
- Work with dates using EOMONTH and DATEDIF.
- Build a PivotTable and use Excel tables.
- Read and fix common errors such as `#N/A` and `#VALUE!`.

**Next step:** INDEX and MATCH, and combining several functions in one formula.

## Advanced

- Use INDEX and MATCH, two-way lookups and lookups on several conditions.
- Use dynamic array functions such as FILTER, SORT and UNIQUE.
- Write SUMPRODUCT for conditions SUMIFS cannot express.
- Name intermediate steps with LET to keep long formulas readable.
- Build a model with separate inputs, calculations and outputs, plus checks.
- Audit someone else's workbook and find the mistakes.
- Know when to move work to Power Query or a PivotTable instead of formulas.

## Describing your level

On a CV, name the functions and tasks rather than a level word. "SUMIFS, XLOOKUP and PivotTables for monthly sales reporting" says more than "advanced Excel", and an interviewer can test it.

## Finding out for sure

The exercises on this site are marked by level 1 to 3, roughly beginner to advanced. Try a few from each [learning track](/exercises/tracks/). The level where you start needing hints is the one to work on.
